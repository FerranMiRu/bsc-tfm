{\large What worked, what didn't, and how to store the next dataset\par}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.15cm}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\hrule
\vspace{0.35cm}

\noindent\fbox{\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}{\small
\textbf{TLDR.} Training was data-loader bound: the H100s sat idle $>$90\% of the time, with a
$\sim$80~s stall every \texttt{num\_workers} batches. The cost was GPFS \emph{metadata latency},
paid per chunk file opened, independent of its size. The multi-zarr layout opened $\sim$93 chunk
files per sample. \textbf{Two fixes:} (1)~stack all variables into one \texttt{dynamic} +
one \texttt{static} array so a sample opens $\sim$9 files, not $\sim$93; (2)~read patches through
raw \texttt{zarr}, bypassing the xarray/dask graph whose per-chunk cost scales
with the store's time extent. On the full historical store, 10 epochs on 1 node went from $\sim$55~h
\ra{} $\sim$2.3~h (merge) \ra{} $\sim$1.3~h (raw reader). Training is now GPU-bound. All layouts are
bit-identical (parity~0), so model accuracy is unchanged.}}

% ============================================================
\section*{1. The bottleneck (diagnosis)}
% ============================================================

Per-sample fetch time factors as \emph{opens/sample} \x{} \emph{cost/open}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Opens/sample is set by the layout.} 41 read calls (one per variable per timestep)
    \x{} $\sim$2.25 chunk files touched per random $256^2$ read $\approx$ 93 opens (measured 92.7).
  \item \textbf{Cost/open is GPFS metadata latency, flat in bytes.} Cold open $\sim$20~ms, warm
    $\sim$0.1~ms. Per-chunk anatomy: bytes-from-disk $\sim$19--20~ms, xarray materialise $\sim$16~ms,
    Blosc decode 0.3~ms.
  \item \textbf{Not a bandwidth problem.} Single-stream GPFS $\sim$556~MB/s; the $\sim$3~MB compressed
    pulled per sample would take $\sim$5~ms if bandwidth-bound, against the $\sim$1.25~s observed
    ($\sim$200\x{} gap).
  \item \textbf{The periodic stall} is structural: the prefetch pool refills all \texttt{num\_workers}
    workers at once, so one slow refill lands every \texttt{num\_workers} batches. More workers hide it
    (deeper outstanding I/O) but never remove it.
  \item \textbf{Store-size effect.} Bigger store \ra{} slower per sample, though the slice read is
    identical. The cause: the xarray/dask graph enumerates the store's \emph{full} time extent, not
    the slice.
\end{itemize}

% ============================================================
\section*{2. What worked}
% ============================================================

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Multi-zarr \ra{} single store.} One store per refill instead of five.
    Stalls shrink $\sim$8\x{} ($\sim$80~s \ra{} $\sim$10~s). However, the stalls remain and they
    increase with store size (the $\sim$80~s \ra{} $\sim$10~s is between the Multizarr 1900 - 2020
    and the single zarr 2000 - 2015).
  \item \textbf{Single \ra{} merged (\texttt{MergedZarrDataset}).} Stack the 14 LUH2 + population +
    K\"oppen--Geiger into one \texttt{dynamic} array (variable as a new axis) and the 6 static into one
    \texttt{static} array. Opens/sample 93~\ra{}~9 ($\sim$10\x{}), file count 16.9\x{} fewer,
    $\sim$7\x{} single-thread. Short-window training becomes GPU-bound.
  \item \textbf{Merged xarray \ra{} raw-zarr reader (\texttt{RawMergedZarrDataset},
    \texttt{dataset\_type: merged\_raw}).} Read patches via \texttt{zarr\_array.oindex[...]}, no
    xarray/dask. Removes the per-chunk Python/GIL graph work (the store-size-dependent term):
    $\sim$7\x{} single-thread over merged-xarray, $\sim$1.9--2.5\x{} multi-worker (the gain grows with
    workers), $\sim$0\% stalls on training. This is the recommended loader.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{3. What was tried and did not work}
% ============================================================

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.5cm}p{10.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Tried} & \textbf{Outcome and reason} \\
\midrule
Disable \texttt{ProcessSynchronizer} &
  No effect. The zarr~v2 read path (\texttt{\_chunk\_getitem}) never acquires the lock; it guards
  writes only. Config toggle removed, instance kept defensively. \\
Offline preprocessing bake-in (fill-NaN, normalize) &
  $\sim$0 gain. Preprocessing was $\sim$8\% of per-sample cost; the residual is
  stack/transpose/reshape, which a bake-in cannot remove. \\
\texttt{ThreadPoolExecutor} over per-variable LUH2 reads &
  No gain; threads=2 regressed $\sim$23\%. Signature of Blosc/GIL contention, not I/O that
  parallelizes this way. \\
\texttt{chunks=None} (drop dask, keep xarray) &
  No multi-worker gain. Keeps xarray's indexing overhead; the win needs xarray removed entirely
  (raw zarr). \\
Chunk shape $\ne 512^2$ &
  Worse. $256^2$ multiplies chunk count and graph work; $1024^2$/$2048^2$ become decode/read-amplified.
  $512^2$ is optimal for $256^2$ patches. \\
NVMe staging (copy store to node-local disk) &
  Discarded. Production store $>$213~GB does not fit the 480~GB local NVMe. \\
Zarr v3 sharding &
  Low value here. Shrinks file count but not the logical chunk count the graph enumerates; our Blosc
  decode is already 0.3~ms/chunk, and GPU-native decode is therefore irrelevant. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

% ============================================================
\section*{4. Full-dataset follow-up (post-thesis)}
% ============================================================

The thesis established the fix on short-window stores and projected that merging alone would keep the
GPUs fed at full scale. It did not, quite. On the full merged store (all variables, full time range,
built by Joan) the $\sim$80~s stalls collapsed to 1--2~s spikes but did not vanish: 10 epochs took
$\sim$2.3~h. The residual is the xarray store-size term (graph cost grows with the store's time extent,
not the slice). Writing the raw-zarr reader removed it and brought the run to $\sim$1.3~h, at which point
the GPU, not the data path, sets the pace. Parity held throughout (bit-identical, so accuracy is
unchanged), and per-GPU throughput is flat from 1 to 16 ranks.

% ============================================================
\section*{5. Knobs to optimize a new dataset}
% ============================================================

Goal: minimize \emph{cold chunk-file opens per sample}, keep chunk files reasonably large, and keep the
store's file count from outgrowing the metadata cache.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.6cm}p{10.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Knob} & \textbf{Target} \\
\midrule
Sanity target           & loader delivery $\ge$ GPU consume ($\sim$53 samples/s/GPU) \\
Verify                  & parity (bit-identical) before any speed claim \\
File opens per sample        & single digits ($\le \sim$10) \\
Chunk file size (compressed) & $\ge \sim$1 MB; avoid $<$100 KB \\
Spatial chunk side $C$  & $\ge 2\times$ patch side $P$; expected chunks/read $=(1+P/C)^2$ \\
Store count             & one ARCO store, not per-modality \\
Variable layout         & dynamic stacked on a variable axis; static stacked separately \\
Reader         & raw zarr (\texttt{oindex}), not xarray \\
Codec                   & Blosc(lz4) fine; decode negligible \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Reference card. When in doubt, cut opens/sample first, it pays off in every cache state.}
\end{table}
